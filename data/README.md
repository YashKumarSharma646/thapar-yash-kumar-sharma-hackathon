# Data

Only public or synthetic data is used. No real client data.

| Folder | Contents | In git? |
|---|---|---|
| `sample/` | Small subsets used by replay mode, so the app runs without any downloads | Yes |
| `raw/` | Full public datasets, fetched by `python scripts/download_data.py` | No |
| `reference/` | Tracked companies, synthetic portfolio, stress-scenario library | Yes |
| `labeling/` | Day 4 distillation corpus and LLM-teacher labels | Yes |
| `processed/` | Generated files (signals and stress-test JSONL, prices, calibration set) | No |

## Replay sample (`sample/`)

Built by `python scripts/build_sample.py` from the raw sets; window **15 Jul – 31 Aug 2018**
(Facebook Q2 earnings crash on 25–26 Jul, US–China tariff escalation, Turkish lira crisis).

| File | Rows | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| `news_2018.csv` | 2,927 | De-duplicated headlines mentioning a tracked company or a macro/geopolitical theme |
| `tweets_2018.csv` | 5,629 | 25% of finance-relevant tweets about tracked companies + 0.5% of spam/chatter (to exercise the noise filter) |

`reference/companies.csv` defines the 30 tracked companies (ticker, sector, aliases).

## Module B reference data

- `reference/portfolio.csv`: synthetic wholesale book (seed 7), regenerate with `python -m ripple.stress_test.portfolio`.
  The brief suggests transaction datasets (retail and card payments). They do not describe a wholesale book of loans,
  bonds and derivatives, so the book is generated instead; ratings, PDs (long-run default rates by grade), spreads
  and durations follow standard market conventions.
- `reference/scenario_library.json`: factor moves measured over real episodes from Yahoo Finance closes (equity ETFs,
  ^TNX, FX, bond ETFs as spread proxies), plus each stock's 2-day abnormal-return volatility (2017–2018).
  Fields that free data cannot measure (regional CDS widening) are labelled as assumptions in the file.

### Data-quality notes
- The tweet dataset's `*_DAY_RETURN` columns are wrong (Facebook shows +23% on 26 Jul 2018, the day it fell ~19%); prices are correct. Returns are recomputed from prices.
- The news dataset's `stock` column is the ticker page a headline was listed on, not its subject. Entities are linked from the headline text.
- `raw_analyst_ratings.csv` mixes timestamp formats; pandas' default date inference silently drops ~96% of its dates. Parse with `format="ISO8601"` (`scripts/build_sample.py: load_news`).
- Only ~9% of company-tagged tweets in the window are finance-relevant; the rest is template spam and chatter.
